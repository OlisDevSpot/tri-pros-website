Open Systems and tap through the systems in each version. The camera move is a stand-in made from the captured picture; in the app the camera glides to the system.

Picked: B

The card sits beside the system it changes, so the homeowner sees the input and the part it changes together. On the phone the card drops in from the top and the system shows below it. A stays below for reference.

How focus behaves

  • Tapping another system moves the camera straight there; the card swaps.
  • Opening Home facts, Modify or Benefits puts Systems away and brings the camera back.
  • A dot on a system chip means the build changes it.
  • Picks stay in the card; the scene changes behind it.

My calls, to check

  • Systems has no dimmed layer: the scene is the point, so it stays bright and you can still turn the camera.
  • Without Done, Modify on the phone closes with a tap above or below it, or Modify in the group.
  • A tap on the scene, away from the group, the rail and the card, puts Systems away and brings the camera back, as a tap outside a modal does. Dragging to turn the camera does not count as a tap.

Real vs stand-in

Real
The scene picture (captured from the lab page), the card's tokens, type and padding, every label and choice (the scene's copy constants), the upgrade rows and their verbs, the flat-roof rule, the 8% cool-roof and 4% attic-fan electric cuts, your default bills, the attic insulation each era starts with and the 1950–1978 fallback (the app's era table), and Recenter's coming and going.
Stand-in
The camera moving to a system (a zoom of the captured picture; the app will glide its camera); Area's and Lot's future inputs; the scene dock. The windows, paint, ducts and crawl each era sets are the app's own era tables. The scene picture does not change when you flip; the real scene does.
Left out
Warranty terms, manufacturer documents (shown as a chip only), CO₂ figures, and voice and natural language (step 3).
