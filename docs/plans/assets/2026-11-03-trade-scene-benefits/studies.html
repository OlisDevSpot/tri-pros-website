Real vs stand-in

Real
The window shell, its tokens, head, tag and bills footer (as built); the systems and their names (HOME_SYSTEM_LABELS); the upgrades the scene installs and their parts (SCOPE_INSTALLS); the bill cuts and how they combine (TRADE_BILL_CUTS, combineCuts: the cool roof 8% electric, the attic 10% and 15%, the radiant barrier 5% and 2%, the attic fan 4% and so on); the upkeep working numbers at Worn (REPAIRS_PER_YEAR: roof $400, windows $200, paint $150, heating and cooling $600 a year); the climate default bills ($200 / $100 inland, $155 / $110 coastal); the claim wording ("about N%", "up to about N%"); the manufacturers you named (Owens Corning Duration COOL, WMB, Cool Life, Goodman).
Draft
Every benefit statement (marked Draft). Each one is a claim, so you approve the wording before it reaches the registry.
Stand-in
The scene behind the window (a captured still); "Open Modify" in the empty state (a toast). Each material’s condition is what the code gives a home of 1950–1978 with nothing recorded (Failing, see the repairs rows); the rep sets the real one in Systems.
Future savings
Real: the ROI calculator's rule (remodel-roi-calculator/lib/project-remodel-roi.ts). Year 1 is the coming twelve months, at today's figures. Each later year grows the bill by its rate once (bill × (1 + rate)year − 1), and repairs grow with construction prices. The total adds up every month from year 1. Its rates are the team's working numbers (REMODEL_ROI_CONFIG_DEFAULTS in remodel-roi-calculator/constants/config-defaults.ts): electric 7.57%, gas 9.1% and construction 5% a year. Its source tags are Working number, Calculated, and Typed for a bill typed in (the calculator's "You told us"). Ruled for the scene: upkeep rises for the whole look-ahead, where the calculator stops an old install's repairs at its replacement year (R218). Calendar names: year N is named by the calendar year it ends in, counted from 2 Nov 2026, so year 5 is 2031 and year 10 is 2036.
Repairs rows
Real: one row per home material that carries repairs (HOME_MATERIALS[id].repairs: roof & gutters, windows, exterior paint, heating & cooling), labelled "option · condition" as trade-scenes/lib/repairs-rows.ts does. The working figure is REPAIRS_PER_YEAR[trade] × REPAIRS_SHARE_BY_WEAR[wear] (lib/repairs-figure.ts: New 0, Good 0.4, Fair 0.7, Worn 1, Failing 1.5) in $ a year; a typed figure wins (repairsOverrides). The condition: the code dates a 1950–1978 home to 1964 (ERA_BUILD_YEAR), and wearForAge on a 62-year-old material is Failing for each of the four (resolve-home-materials.ts). After the work, repairs end only on a material the build draws a part of (landed() in lib/scene-savings.ts). Stand-in: the shingle roof and central air are taken as recorded, since the build’s picks imply them.
Two futures
Without the build is the costs before the work, and with the build the costs after it, from the same costs engine and the same rates as round 4. Colour: with the build is the build’s blue (--m-primary), without is a gray (--m-without), and the saving is the success tint; checked with the dataviz palette validator on the window’s surface (colour-blind ΔE 17.2, normal 19.2, contrast pass). The chart starts at $0. In the build: recharts 3 in ChartContainer with a static ChartConfig naming both series, and the picked year as the active index driven by the calendar (the app’s pinned touch-tooltip recipe).
Motion
Mapped onto motion/react v12 for the build. Today’s costs and the popover enter and leave through AnimatePresence (opacity and a small translate or scale from the anchor). The rail’s swap is a keyed child under AnimatePresence mode="popLayout" in a fixed-size box. The chart’s bracket and the lit column are plain CSS transitions on transform-friendly properties. The readout figures count with useMotionValue and animate() in tabular figures at a fixed width. Round 7’s width change: the rail’s wrapper is a motion.div animating width (0 ↔ 380 px, 240 ms) inside AnimatePresence, and the matrix is a layout sibling with fixed row heights, so its columns reflow every frame and no cell snaps. The rail’s content keeps its own 380 px width and fades in after the wrapper starts. One easing, [0.16, 1, 0.3, 1], at 160–280 ms; useReducedMotion lands everything at once. Per the app’s trap: motion goes on the slotted child, never on a Button asChild parent, and no surface gets two opacity entrances.
Left out
Warranty terms (no term exists in the company constants yet; the Tri Pros workmanship warranty is named without one), manufacturer documents, CO₂ figures, colours.
